Why the conversion isn't one to one

The two systems count different things. That is the whole reason a conversion factor exists, and it is why every honest converter returns an approximation rather than an exact figure.

Divide the two full-time years into each other and you get the familiar 2:1 ratio: 60 ECTS a year against 30 semester credits a year. So one US semester credit converts to 2 ECTS, and 2 ECTS convert back to one US semester credit. Compare raw workload hours instead and the ratio lands somewhere between 1.5:1 and 1.8:1, depending on whether your institution counts an ECTS credit as 30 hours or 25. All three figures are defensible, but 2:1 is the one evaluators and admissions offices actually apply, so it is the default here.

Questions students actually ask

How many ECTS is 1 US semester credit?
Two ECTS. The ratio falls out of the full-time load on each side: 30 US semester credits is one US academic year, 60 ECTS is one European academic year.
Why isn't the conversion one to one?
The two systems count different things. One ECTS credit represents 25 to 30 hours of total student workload, independent study included. One US semester credit is built from classroom contact time 鈥?one hour of instruction plus at least two hours of outside work per week across roughly fifteen weeks, or about 45 hours in total. Because the units measure different activity, any conversion is an approximation rather than an exact equivalence.
How do I convert US quarter credits to ECTS?
Convert quarter credits to semester credits first by dividing by 1.5, then double the result. One semester credit equals 1.5 quarter credits, so 45 quarter credits is 30 semester credits, which is 60 ECTS.
I have 120 US semester credits. Is that a full European bachelor's?
It is about 240 ECTS, which aligns with a four-year European bachelor's (240 ECTS). The gap to watch is the other direction: a three-year Bologna bachelor's is 180 ECTS, roughly 90 US semester credits against the 120鈥?30 a US degree requires.
What if my university uses a different ratio?
Use it. Published institutional ratios override the general 2:1 baseline. Ratios of 1.5:1 and 3:1 both appear in practice, usually where contact hours are unusually high or low.
Can I submit a converted credit total to a university?
No. Calculator results are estimates for planning. Formal credit recognition is decided by the receiving institution, usually through a Learning Agreement for exchange study or a credential evaluation for degree admission. Use a converted figure to plan and to ask better questions, then confirm with the institution.
Does earning more US credits raise my European qualification level?
No. Degree recognition depends on the qualification actually awarded (Bachelor, Master), not on credit volume. Extra credits above a degree's requirement do not convert a bachelor's into a master's.
Which countries use ECTS?
All members of the European Higher Education Area 鈥?the EU states plus Iceland, Norway, Turkey and others. A number of universities outside Europe have adopted it as well.
