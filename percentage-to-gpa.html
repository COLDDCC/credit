Why there isn't one formula

A percentage is a single number. A US GPA is shorthand for the average of individual course grades, weighted by credit. Slope a straight line between them and you get 梅25 鈥?but that assumes a 100% is four points of anything, which isn't how grades work in any country.

China / India
90 = strong

In China an 85 is typically solid work and 90 is excellent; in India an 85 in engineering is genuinely strong. Evaluators map these bands up rather than flattening them against a US curve.

US 4.0 letters
90 = B

On a typical US letter scale 90+ is an A and 80鈥?9 is a B (3.0). A Chinese 85 converting to a US B by raw letter would badly understate the same work in context.

That mismatch is the whole reason credential evaluators exist and why they work course by course. WES, ECE and their peers read the transcript, the institution, and the grading culture together 鈥?and their output varies by institution, which is why no single published band table is authoritative. The three methods above are an honest picture of the range you're working with, not a claim to a single truth.

Questions students actually ask

How do I convert my percentage to a 4.0 GPA?
There is no single formula. The quick rule of thumb divides by 25 (85% 鈫?3.4), but that flattens the top of the scale. Credential evaluators such as WES convert course by course using band tables that vary by country and institution.
Is 梅25 accurate for Chinese students?
It is a rough estimate only. Chinese universities commonly use threshold tables 鈥?for example 90鈥?00 mapping to the 4.0 area and 85鈥?9 to the 3.7鈥?.6 area 鈥?that behave differently from 梅25. Use your university's published conversion when it exists, and expect WES to do its own thing.
Is 85% a good GPA for a US master's?
Roughly a 3.5 by the simple rule, and varies by band mapping. Most US master's programmes want a 3.0 or better; selective ones look for 3.5+. Context matters more than the raw number.
Does WES publish its conversion table?
No. WES converts each course using its own institutional methodology, exposed through the iGPA calculator and paid evaluations. Published band tables, including this page's, are reconstructions of common practice.
Should I report a self-calculated GPA to a university?
Report exactly what your transcript says first. Most US programmes either take it as-is or require a professional evaluation. If you convert, state the method next to the number rather than presenting it as official.
Why do China and India convert differently from the US scale?
Grading cultures differ: a 90 in the US often means 'B', while a 90 in China or India is typically strong work. Evaluators adjust for this, which is why the same percentage maps higher in some systems than a raw percentage-to-letter mapping would suggest.
