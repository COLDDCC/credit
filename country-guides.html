Grading systems, by country

A 7 in the Netherlands is not a 70%. A 2.5 in Germany is not a 2.5 in the US. Every country's grades carry a grading culture along with a scale 鈥?and converting without knowing that culture produces numbers that look wrong to the people reading them. Why grading culture matters

Same number, different meaning
7 鈮?70%

In the Netherlands a 7 is solid, respectable work 鈥?a "good". Treating it as 70% and mapping that to a US C would badly understate the same transcript.

Direction flips
2.5 = good

German grades run the other way: 1.0 is the best you can get and 4.0 is the minimum pass. A 2.5 is comfortably in the "good" band, not a middle-of-the-road 2.5.

That is why every country guide here converts three ways 鈥?the simple rule of thumb, a WES-style band mapping, and a country-specific approach 鈥?and labels each. Evaluators (WES, ECE and their peers) read the transcript, the institution and the grading culture together, and their output varies by institution. No single published table is authoritative; the range is the honest answer.
